As it turned out, there weren’t all that many ways to be a superhero.

To successfully be one—to actually, effectively intervene. For a brief and glorious moment, Conor had done the thing, had been doing the thing—been in it, felt it, lived it, the fulfillment of a lifetime of dreaming and almost six months of preparation. It had been like breaking the Gap, only better, bolder, purer. He had been right all along:

This is what life should be like.

The buzz had stayed with him all the way through to the next morning, when he had gotten off the bus, the mask in his pocket, full of hope and determination…

…only to have it all drain away over the course of the day.

The trick with the bags had been serendipitous, a perfect way to disrupt the bullies’ game, buy their victims time to escape—but opportunities like that were few and far between. Most of the time, the crime was already over by the time Conor came across the aftermath, the perpetrator long gone and usually anonymous. And when he did see awful things happening in real time, there was often no obvious way to stop them.

Well, I mean…

Okay, sure, he could almost always have stopped them by just…inserting himself into the middle of things, the way he and Ashleigh had on that first afternoon, when Eichardt had been taking his revenge on Timothy.

But for one thing, that would mostly just mean getting his face stomped in. For another, it wasn’t great for staying under the radar.

(Not to mention that they’d usually just get right back to it.)

There were some crimes of opportunity where delaying things, bringing down more attention, might mean that the whole thing ended and didn’t restart. But there were plenty where trying to intervene would do nothing more than briefly inconvenience the bad guys—if that.

What if what you really need is, I dunno, Tae Kwon Do? Or CPR? Or how to hold your breath for a really long time?

Ashleigh’s words, half-remembered from the rooftop. They’d already hit hard, at the time, and now it had been a full three days since the incident with the bags, and Conor had saved zero additional people.
