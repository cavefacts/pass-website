“Yeah?” said one of the other figures.

“Go look over the edge of this roof and tell me what you see. On the other rooftop.”

The figure moved.

“Could be,” he called out. “I mean. I ain’t no expert. But it sure does look like some footprints. Gravel’s all fucked up in about the right spot. And it’s in line with this line.”

The gun-wielder gave a low whistle. Another one of the figures nudged D.G., murmuring something Conor couldn’t quite catch. “D…kid you…about? …Spiderman shit?”

(More wild, despairing laughter.)

“No product anywhere on this roof, cap,” called out another of the figures, closing a panel on one of the fans. “Smoke screen. Fucker lied to us.”

“I’m telling you, it was here! The kid must have—”

The thud was harsher this time, meatier, and there was a whoosh of breath followed by violent retching, as the fucking mouth breather Snigs curled into a ball and D.G. raised the baseball bat back to his shoulder.

“You got a bag somewhere on your person, kid?” asked the gun-wielder, in the tone of an afterthought, a formality.

Conor shook his head.

“You throw one off the roof?”

“No.”

“Fuckin’ Snigs,” the gun-wielder muttered, under his breath. “Leave two large of product on the roof of a goddamn school, what the fuck do you expect.” He turned his head, raised his voice. “Your shit didn’t get ganked by a middle schooler, asshole, some fucking contractor came up here and struck gold.”

The gun-wielder turned again. “D.G., this is your shit. How you want to handle this?”

D.G. shifted, his head turning, the moonlight shining past the hood of his jacket—

Of course.

(The laughter was almost hysterical, now.)

It was Devon Green.

“Kid,” Devon said, and Conor could hear it now, the half-familiar voice—Devon without the stage, without the mask, like he had sounded on the bus only somehow grimmer, emptier, more dangerous. The bat resting on his shoulder spun slowly as he twisted it in one hand. “How’d you get here?”

Kid—

Conor opened his mouth, tried to think of something to say, surely there was something he could say, surely Devon would help—had he not recognized Conor yet?

The older boy cleared his throat.

Answer the question.

“Bike,” Conor said.

“Parents know you’re here?”

“I snuck out.”

Hey, maybe we should’ve lied about that one—

Devon’s gaze flickered toward the trapdoor, and then to Snigs, still wheezing on the gravel. “We could take him with us for a bit,” he said. “Impress upon him the need for discretion.”

The gun-wielder snorted, spat. “No,” he said. “I’m fucking tired. This whole shit is a wash, I’m not dragging this out. We deal with this and we go home.”

He fixed his gaze on Conor. “What do you think?” he asked, his voice now louder, colder. “Throw him off the fuckin’ roof?”

Conor’s heart leapt into his throat as sweat broke out across his entire body.

I’m fifteen feet away from the edge, now, if I turn, if I run, I can make it—

But the gun.

Devon’s face didn’t twitch, didn’t shift, was a mask of iron in the moonlight. “Not really feeling it, cap. Seems a bit overkill. Ain’t like he knows shit, even if he’s inclined to talk.”

“Still,” the gun-wielder said idly. “Loose fuckin’ ends.”

Conor could feel himself panicking, unraveling—

Don’t white out, do not white out, you can not afford to white out they will kill you—

“Devon,” he said, his voice trembling. “Please—”

The older boy exploded into motion, terrifyingly fast. The bat slammed down onto the gravel, sending up sparks, making Conor jump, and by the time his feet touched down, the older boy was in his face, surging forward, pressing, looming.

“The fuck you say, motherfucker?” he snarled, his eyes burning in the moonlight. “You say something to me? You say somebody’s name?”

Conor staggered back a step, then another, and then another, a tiny part of his brain trying to track how close he was getting to the edge while the rest came apart in shreds and terror.

“What’s my name, bitch?” Devon demanded. “You know me? You fucking know me? Who the fuck am I?”

Conor opened his mouth. “I—”

The bat slammed into the gravel again, this time close enough that Conor felt the sting of shrapnel in the skin of his neck, his jaw.

“Who am I, cocksucker?” Devon repeated. “You sounded like you knew somebody. Say it, motherfucker, what’s my name?”

“I—I don’t know—”

—what you want, he had been going to say, I don’t know what you want from me, but the older boy put a hand flat on his chest and shoved him back still further. “Fuckin’ right, asshole,” he said, cutting Conor off. “You don’t know who the fuck I am. You don’t know shit.”

Devon turned, looked back at the gun-wielder, nostrils flared, chest heaving. “I say we just roll, cap,” he growled. “Get the fuck out of here. Conor fuckin’ Moreton ain’t planning on telling nobody shit.”

Slowly—too slowly—Conor’s brain was putting it together, realizing his mistake, the window of opportunity he had almost accidentally slammed shut, the threat that Devon was now making. He opened his mouth—

No. Say nothing. Do nothing.

The gun-wielder was studying him, his own eyes cold.

“Nah,” he said, sounding almost bored. “Not convinced.” He drew the gun from his belt again, holding it down low, one finger tapping on the side of the trigger guard. “Kid doesn’t look scared enough, to me.”

Conor had never been more scared in his entire life.

“—what’s to stop him following us, or trying to call the cops?”

Devon shrugged. “Easy fix for that,” he said.

And then he turned, and swung the bat, and there was pain.

What were you expecting?

…honestly, not this.

Broken, his arm was almost certainly broken, was a roaring maelstrom of pain and had already begun to swell from shoulder to elbow.

The six figures were gone, had disappeared at some point, gone back through the trapdoor and left him there alone. And now it was cold, and getting colder, and Conor could barely think, could barely see, had at some point thrown up on himself and was curled up into a tiny ball, trying not to move.

Ashleigh, call Ashleigh, Ashleigh is right down there—

No, don’t call him, they’ll see him, they’ll get him—

You have to WARN him—

But it was too much, was too hard to move. He managed to uncurl himself, throwing up again in the process, the pain flaring back into white fire, only to realize that his phone was gone, that they had taken it, must have taken it out of his pocket while his brain was offline.

What were you expecting?

Not this, not this, not this, not this—

Broken. His arm was broken.

And—

And—

And he was stuck up here, he wasn’t going anywhere, he was going to die, of hypothermia and shock, except no he wasn’t, that wouldn’t happen, that couldn’t happen—

(Guyler’s blood staining the sidewalk, a would-be rapist screaming and screaming—)

(That was when he realized that he was screaming, that he had been screaming the whole time, or keening anyway, a sort of strangled, animal squeal that filled the air with every breath, cutting through the silence, my god, this hurt, it hurt so bad, he’d never felt anything this painful before in his entire life.)

Just paint.

“Conor?”

Ashleigh.

“Conor!”

The sound of shoes tearing through gravel, and then his best friend’s face in the moonlight, bloodless and trembling.

Okay…

…safe now.

Conor let go, and disappeared.
