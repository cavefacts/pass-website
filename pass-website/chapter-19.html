Only after what might have been a full minute did Conor dare to look, to look away for the briefest glance, and what he saw—

(Before his eyes snapped right back onto Oliver, confirmed that the monster still slumbered.)

—was Ashleigh, kneeling, leaning, both hands pressed against Guyler’s thigh with what looked like all of his weight, all of his might, his arms were trembling almost as hard as Conor’s were and still the red was leaking, leaking out, leaking through.

“Tourniquet,” Conor heard himself say, the words muffled as if by ten blankets.

“If I move my hands—”

Conor stepped over, blindly, clumsily, picking his way backwards across the shrapnel-scattered floor by feel, the shaking gun held always on the crumpled, unconscious body.

“Put my foot on it,” he said, lifting one leg. “My toe.”

Two hands guided his foot into place, pressed it down. Conor shifted, focusing almost all of his weight onto that one tiny spot.

There was a brief scramble, the leg shifting under Conor’s foot as the other boy threaded a belt under it, pulling it tight—

“Okay,” Ashleigh said, and then he collapsed.

“Ashleigh?” Conor called out.

There was no answer.

“Ashleigh!”
